# CTA Experiment — which call-to-action reads as most persuasive

Bonus challenge: *experiment with different CTAs to see which feels most persuasive.*

For each page I wrote three CTA variants, judged them against the business goal, and shipped the strongest one. The two dimensions I weighed were **copy** (does the wording lower the perceived effort and match intent?) and **placement** (is the CTA in front of the visitor at every decision point?).

## Bark & Bubbles (dog grooming) — goal: booking

| Variant | Copy | Read |
|---|---|---|
| A | **"Book an appointment"** | Clear, action-first, names the exact next step. Zero ambiguity about what happens on click. |
| B | "Check availability" | Softer, lower-commitment, but adds a mental step ("check… then what?") before the goal. |
| C | "Get started" | Friendly but vague — doesn't say *book*, so it leaks intent. |

**Winner: A — "Book an appointment."** For a booking goal, naming the action beats softening it; pet owners arriving on a grooming page already intend to book, so the clearest verb converts best. The nav uses the shorter **"Book now"** so the sticky bar stays compact while the hero and closing band use the full phrase.

**Placement decision:** the CTA repeats in the **nav, hero, every service card, and the closing band** — so it's never more than one scroll-length away. Each click opens the booking modal on the page rather than navigating away, removing the biggest drop-off point.

## Sweeplight (home cleaning) — goal: quote requests

| Variant | Copy | Read |
|---|---|---|
| A | **"Get a free quote"** | "Free" removes price risk; "quote" sets the expectation (no payment, no commitment). Perfect for a lead-gen goal. |
| B | "Request a cleaning" | Implies booking/paying now — heavier than the visitor is ready for at this stage. |
| C | "Contact us" | Generic, and signals a phone call/email chore, which is exactly the friction we're trying to remove. |

**Winner: A — "Get a free quote."** The word *free* plus *quote* explicitly promises a low-commitment, no-payment path, which matches a quote-inquiry goal far better than anything that hints at booking or a phone call. It's reinforced by the "2 minutes to a quote" proof stat right beside it.

**Placement decision:** the CTA repeats in the **nav, hero, all three pricing tiers, and the closing band**, and opens a two-minute quote modal instead of forcing a call — keeping the visitor on the page through the moment of intent.

## Takeaway

The pattern that won on both pages: **name the exact next action in the CTA, match the commitment level to the goal (book vs. free quote), and repeat the button at every decision point with an on-page modal so there's no navigation friction.** The vaguer or heavier variants consistently added a mental step between the visitor and the goal.
